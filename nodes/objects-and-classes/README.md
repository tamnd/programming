This area is about objects and the ways that languages let objects share behaviour. It covers classes and prototypes, inheritance and composition, interfaces, and the different kinds of dispatch.

## What it holds

- Objects: objects as state with behaviour, and identity.
- Classes: classes as templates for objects, fields, methods and static members.
- Prototypes: objects that inherit directly from other objects, as in JavaScript.
- Encapsulation: access control and the boundary between the inside and the outside of an object.
- Inheritance: single and multiple inheritance, mixins and their problems.
- Interfaces and protocols: contracts that a type promises to meet, with or without an explicit declaration.
- Polymorphism and dispatch: single, double and multiple dispatch, virtual methods and overloading.
- Composition over inheritance: building objects from other objects instead of from a base class.
- Constructors and initialization: constructors, initialization order and factory functions.
- Object lifecycle: creation, use, finalization and destruction of an object.
- The expression problem: adding new types and new operations without changing old code.

## What it does not hold

- Design patterns, such as visitor or strategy. They are in [Software design](/software-design/).
- Memory management of objects, such as garbage collection. It is in [Memory and resources](/memory-and-resources/).
- Subtyping and variance as type system ideas. They are in [Types](/types/).
- The object-oriented style as a whole. It is in [Paradigms](/paradigms/).
