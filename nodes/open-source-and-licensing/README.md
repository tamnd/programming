This area covers free and open-source software and the legal rules that programmers meet. It holds a card for each SPDX licence, and pages on how open-source projects work and how to contribute.

## What it holds

- **Free and open-source software.** The ideas, the definitions and the history of free software and open source, and how the two movements differ.
- **Licence concepts.** The ideas that every licence page uses: what a licence grants, permissive and copyleft terms, compatibility and exceptions. The sub-area is split from licences, because the concepts and the families are different kinds of page.
- **Licences.** One page per main licence family, with its versions, its terms and its use.
- **Licence compliance.** Meeting the terms of the licences of the code a program uses and ships: identifiers, notices, scans and policies. The sub-area is new, because compliance is a practice of its own and it holds the core item spdx.
- **Contribution practice.** How to contribute to an open-source project, and how a project accepts work from others.
- **Governance and foundations.** How open-source projects make decisions, and the foundations that host projects and hold their assets.
- **Open source sustainability.** How open-source projects stay alive: money, people and the load on maintainers. The sub-area is new, because the topic has its own sources and does not fit governance.
- **Copyright and patents for programmers.** Copyright, patents, trademarks and trade secrets as a programmer meets them. The pages explain the rules; they do not give legal advice.
- **Export rules.** Export controls and sanctions that apply to software and to open-source projects, at the level of an overview. The sub-area is small by nature.

## What it does not hold

- Legal advice for a case. The pages explain the rules. They are not legal advice.
- Supply-chain security of dependencies. It is in [Secure programming](/security/).
- Package managers. They are in [Tools and workflow](/tools-and-workflow/).
