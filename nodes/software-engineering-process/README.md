This area is about how teams plan, build, release and maintain software. It covers requirements, estimation, methods, releases, documentation and the known laws of software projects.

## What it holds

- **Process models.** The models of how software work moves from idea to release, from waterfall to iterative methods. This sub-area is the base for methodologies.
- **Methodologies.** The named methods for running a software team: Agile and its families, Scrum, Kanban, XP and Lean.
- **Requirements in practice.** Finding out what a system must do, writing it down, and keeping it true as the system changes.
- **Estimation.** Estimating the size and the time of software work, and using estimates without abusing them.
- **Planning and tracking.** Turning goals into plans, tracking work and risk, and the issue trackers that hold the work.
- **Releases and versioning.** How software is released, how versions are numbered, and how long each release is supported.
- **Maintenance.** Keeping software working, current and changeable after its first release, and retiring it when its time comes.
- **Quality and reliability.** Software quality and reliability as goals of the process: how teams define them, plan for them and measure them. The testing techniques are in testing, and SRE practice is in distributed-and-cloud.
- **Engineering metrics.** Measures of a team and its output, how to use them, and how they go wrong.
- **Documentation.** Project documentation beyond the code: how to plan, write and keep it. Comments, READMEs and changelogs are in construction-practice; design documents and ADRs are in software-design.
- **Team practice.** How engineering teams are formed, work together and stay healthy.
- **Laws of software.** The named observations about software projects, with their source, their exact words and the evidence for them.
- **Ethics and professional practice.** The ethics of building software, the codes of the profession, and the responsibility of programmers for what their programs do.

## What it does not hold

- Code-level practice, such as code review. It is in [Construction practice](/construction-practice/).
- CI and CD tools. They are in [Tools and workflow](/tools-and-workflow/).
- Licences and open-source governance. They are in [Open source and licensing](/open-source-and-licensing/).
- Business management that has no link to software. It is out of scope.
